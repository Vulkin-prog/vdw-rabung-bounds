\section*{AI contribution statement}\label{s:ai}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{AI contribution statement}

The research reported here was carried out in collaboration with AI systems
(Anthropic's Claude, models \texttt{claude-fable-5} and \texttt{claude-opus-4-8}),
which contributed at a level well beyond copy-editing. Following the practice now
emerging in this literature, we state precisely what the models did, what the
author did, and---most importantly---how the results can be checked without
trusting either.

\paragraph{What the AI systems did.}
Under the author's direction, the models wrote the GPU scanning engine
(\texttt{scan\_gpu.cu}) and the independent verifiers, designed and executed the
campaign of Section~\ref{s:campaign}, carried out the statistical analysis of
Section~\ref{s:density}, and produced the first draft of this paper. They also
performed the pre-announcement literature scan discussed below.

\paragraph{What the author did.}
The author conceived the project and set its scope; made every decision about
what to compute, when to stop, and what to claim; provided and operated the
hardware; and supplied adversarial challenges at each stage, several of which
caught substantive errors that the automated review had missed. The author takes
full responsibility for the entire contents of this paper, including the data,
the references, and the conclusions, irrespective of how any part of it was
generated.

\paragraph{How the results can be checked.}
We regard the following apparatus, and not any assurance about the tools used,
as the basis on which the bounds can be relied upon once the release gates in
Section~\ref{s:data} are complete.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Certificates outside the pipeline.} Each bound is certified by
\texttt{verify\_claim}, which recomputes the coloring from scratch in time
$O(p\log p)$ and shares no code with the search engine. A reader who trusts
nothing about our software can inspect or reimplement this verifier
(Section~\ref{s:method} reports its measured cost on our hardware: seconds
to minutes per bound, $O(p)$ memory).
\item \emph{Dependency-aware redundancy.} The mirror and fused full scans share
source and arithmetic, so we treat them as redundant paths, not independent
implementations.  The principal separation comes from the CPU criterion,
\texttt{verify\_claim}, and the Montgomery-free high-range witness; the exact
dependency matrix is Table~\ref{tab:dependencies}.  Final verdicts are parsed
fail-closed and their digests engage the claim identity rather than only a
max-run profile.
\item \emph{Preregistration.} The quantitative forecasts of
Section~\ref{s:density} were committed to the repository before the corresponding
data existed, and read at four fixed cumulative checkpoints
(Section~\ref{s:prereg}).  These readings form one correlated trajectory, not
four replications; their commit hashes are in the repository history.
\item \emph{Novelty, not just correctness.} Because literature-search errors can
invalidate novelty claims, we audited priority separately rather than assuming
it. Our pre-announcement scan located an unpublished
two-colour computation that had gone further than ours for $r=2$; rather than
claim those bounds, we verified its primes with our own certificate machinery and
credit them to their author (Section~\ref{s:bounds}). Every bibliographic
reference in the release bibliography is restricted to an entry actually cited
by this paper.  Each locator is checked against a publisher record or the
primary document, and a machine-readable public qualification report records
the source used for that check without embedding conversational review notes
in the bibliography.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Raw traces.}
The campaign logs, structured claim manifests, source identities, and public
qualification reports are required components of the immutable release.
Internal conversational journals and adversarial-review transcripts are not
scientific evidence and are excluded from the public artifact.  As
Section~\ref{s:data} records, the archive and its DOI have not yet been frozen;
the private preparation repository is not represented as the final audit
package.
