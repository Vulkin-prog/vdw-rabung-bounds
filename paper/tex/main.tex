\begin{abstract}
We report a single-GPU scan of power-residue colorings over all primes in
$[9.7\times10^{8},\,2\times10^{9})$.  It produces direct Rabung certificates
for $W(3,k)$, $17\le k\le25$, headed by
$W(3,17)>31\,385\,622\,833$.  After automatically closing the registered
published and archived baselines under the recurrences of
Blankenship--Cummings--Taranchuk and Xu, the direct bounds remain the strongest
bounds located in that corpus for $17\le k\le21$; the rows $22\le k\le25$ are
direct-method improvements, not general van der Waerden records.  We also
independently verify four archived two-colour certificates.  Applying the
Blankenship--Cummings--Taranchuk recurrence to them gives
$W(3,25)>628\,673\,328\,287$ and, respectively for $k=26,27,28$, the further
bounds $1\,221\,912\,599\,848$, $2\,068\,976\,009\,617$, and
$2\,159\,051\,058\,266$.  Each direct computational claim is represented by a
succinct Rabung certificate $(p,r,k)$ whose hypotheses are re-established by a
stand-alone verifier.  As a secondary empirical result, one frozen forecast is
compared with a cumulative holdout trajectory at four predetermined dates; the
disjoint increments are the primary summary and all Poisson calculations are
reported conditionally on that model.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Provenance.} The scanning software, the campaign, the analysis, and

operated by the author. Every direct computational claim reported here can be
rechecked by a separate-source verifier that shares no code with the search
engine and runs in time $O(p\log p)$. See the AI contribution
statement (p.~\pageref{s:ai}).
\end{abstract}
