\section{The GPU campaign}\label{s:campaign}

\subsection{Fused character evaluation}\label{ss:b7}

The scan kernel exploits the fact that the additive order of positions is free:
we iterate $x=1,2,\dots,p-1$ directly and evaluate the color of $x$ on the fly
by modular exponentiation, fused with the run-length bookkeeping. Nothing is
sorted and no per-element value is materialized in memory. A single pass
evaluates $y=x^{(p-1)/m}$ with $m=\operatorname{lcm}\{\,r\le 9 : r \mid p-1\,\}
\le 2520$; a table lookup of $y$ among the $m$-th roots of unity gives
$\operatorname{ind}(x) \bmod m$, hence the color for every modulus $r\le 9$
simultaneously. Runs are summarized per segment by (prefix, suffix, best
interior) triples combined with an associative, order-respecting reduction.

A second symmetry halves the work. Since $c(p-x)=c(x)+c(-1)$, the second half
of the sequence is the reversal of the first half shifted by the constant
$c(-1)$; it suffices to scan $x=1,\dots,(p-1)/2$ and to splice the two halves
at the center. We refer to this half-interval variant as the mirror scan.

\subsection{Validation by redundant and independent paths}\label{ss:validation}

Correctness is supported by cross-agreement among redundant paths and by
separate-source checkers rather than by trust in any single path. On all primes $p<105\,000$ (both residue classes of $p \bmod 4$ and varied
$m$; the $r$-range of each comparison set is given with it):
\begin{itemize}
\item the sort-based GPU pipeline (an earlier, algorithmically distinct GPU
  design retained as a redundant path) agrees with the CPU reference on $50\,105$
  comparisons ($r\in[2,6]$) with zero disagreements;
\item the fused multi-$r$ kernel agrees with the CPU reference on $27\,508$
  comparisons ($r\in[2,9]$) with zero disagreements (and the specialized $r=2,3$ variant on a
  further $9\,068$);
\item the mirror scan, the fused full scan, the sort-based pipeline, and the
  CPU reference agree on $33\,327$ comparisons over $10^{4}$ primes with zero
  disagreements.
\end{itemize}
These comparison totals and the performance measurements below are historical
pre-flight records.  Their exact raw GPU outputs and benchmark logs must still
be recovered from the campaign PC and bound to the release candidate; the
portable staging suite exercises the algorithms but does not treat these
totals as release-qualified evidence.
For $r=2$ a CPU Jacobi-symbol evaluation provides one further independent
route. This redundancy is not decorative: during pre-flight testing the
cross-check caught a genuine bug (a non-commutative run-summary operator fed to
a reduction that assumes commutativity, producing phantom runs at
$p\sim 1.8\times 10^{9}$); the repaired, order-respecting reduction is covered
by a regression test that has run at every commit since.

\subsection{Measured throughput}\label{ss:throughput}

On the target hardware (one consumer RTX~5090, CUDA, 32-bit Montgomery
arithmetic) the mirror multi-$r$ kernel was benchmarked at
$4.86\times10^{10}$ physical evaluations of $x$ per second at
$p\approx1.2\times10^9$.  Mirror reconstruction makes this equivalent to
$9.72\times10^{10}$ positions of the full sequence per second.  The same run
performed $2.09\times10^{12}$ modular multiplications per second, $61\%$ of the
measured pure-multiplication plateau ($3.40\times10^{12}$).  Under the
$431$\,W campaign power cap the observed kernel throughput was
$4.2$--$4.45\times10^{10}$ physical evaluations, or
$8.4$--$8.9\times10^{10}$ reconstructed full-sequence positions, per second.
These are active-kernel rates.  The ten-day campaign duration is wall time and
also includes sieving, host work, checkpointing, cross-checks, and restarts; we
do not quote a wall-clock modular-multiplication rate. Several plausible
optimizations (e.g.\ a 2-adic factorization of the exponentiation) were
implemented, measured, and rejected as memory-bound regressions.

\subsection{Campaign execution}\label{ss:execution}

According to the preserved campaign design and summaries, the production run
was configured to scan every prime in
$[9.70\times 10^{8},\, 2.0\times 10^{9})$ (each prime tested for every
$r\le 9$ dividing $p-1$), starting just above the largest
published prime $969\,347\,371$ of Monroe's distributed scan~\cite{Monroe2026},
in a single multi-$r$ pass ($r\le 9$). (The narrow interval
$[969\,347\,371,\,9.70\times10^{8})$ was therefore not scanned; it is immaterial,
since any valid prime in it would give a bound below every one we report.) The
historical checkpoint reports that the range was processed in $515$
restartable chunks of width $2\times 10^{6}$, each with a fixed walltime cap.
The registered revision map spans \emph{eight} git revisions and \emph{four}
scanner-source fingerprints.  Their exact allocation and the scientific
effect of each transition are generated from the audit registry below.

\paragraph{Exhaustiveness of the prime enumeration.}
The per-prime computation is validated by the redundancy above; the
\emph{enumeration} of primes deserves its own audit, since the density claims of
Section~\ref{s:density} are claims about \emph{all} primes in the window. The
scanner enumerates primes with a plain in-process sieve per chunk; chunks are
contiguous by construction ($[\,\mathrm{lo}+2i\cdot10^{6}$,
$\mathrm{lo}+2(i{+}1)\cdot10^{6}\,)$), and the checkpoint protocol resumes only
at chunk boundaries, so interruptions can neither skip nor duplicate primes
within the range. We verified the retained aggregate coverage counts against a
separately written segmented sieve with no shared code:
over the $498$ chunks for which per-chunk histograms were logged (the histogram
feature was added at an early restart, from $p=1.004\times10^{9}$ on), the
campaign's per-chunk counts of primes in every congruence class $p\equiv1\pmod
r$, $2\le r\le9$---$3\,984$ counts in all---agree with the independent sieve
\emph{exactly}, with zero discrepancies ($47\,181\,875$ primes on those
chunks). For the first $17$ chunks, which predate the histogram feature,
per-class counts were not logged during the campaign; that range,
$[9.70\times10^{8},1.004\times10^{9})$, was therefore re-scanned after the
campaign in one aggregate invocation with the post-campaign audit build
(commit \texttt{c81383885bd9}; full identities are in the registry).  The eight
independent counts were committed before that invocation completed
(commit \texttt{30e8b83df3c0}).  A version-specific,
fail-closed parser now checks the preserved transcript, whose SHA-256 is
\texttt{dff4e409\ldots de184};
it verifies every historical TARGET, candidate, COLQ, HISTO, and TOP row before
comparing the eight histogram sums with those precommitted counts.

The re-scan also lists exactly $405$ run-valid $(3,17)$ candidates, consistently
with its TARGET row and its $r=3$ histogram.  It was one invocation over the
whole interval, however, so it supplies aggregate class totals rather than
$17\times8$ per-chunk cells.  These audits establish exact agreement of the
counts; counts alone do not establish
identity of the ordered prime lists.  The audit utility therefore also emits a
canonical ordered stream for byte comparison and hashing.  Populating its
per-chunk identity manifest for all $515$ chunks is a release gate, not a
consequence inferred from the $3\,984$ matching counts.  The four historical
scanner blobs contain a byte-identical $249$-byte \texttt{sieve\_primes}
function (SHA-256 recorded in the registry).  That function accepted an
inclusive upper endpoint, whereas the audit stream is half-open; every campaign
boundary is an even integer greater than two, hence composite, so the two
conventions enumerate exactly the same primes on all $515$ chunks.  The current
scanner makes the half-open convention explicit.  In total the window
$[9.7\times10^{8},2\times10^{9})$ contains $48\,823\,489$ primes in the
independent sieve.  Across the retained $498$-chunk histograms and the aggregate
17-chunk re-scan, the preserved outputs report corresponding per-class totals;
full byte-level ordered-prime identity remains a release gate.  The independent
sieve counts $1\,448\,736$ primes in
$[9.7\times10^{8},10^{9})$, hence $47\,374\,753$ in
$[10^{9},2\times10^{9})$.  This equals the externally published difference
$\pi(2\times10^{9})-\pi(10^{9})=98\,222\,287-50\,847\,534$ from Oliveira e
Silva's table~\cite{OliveiraSilvaPi}; it checks the interval difference, not the
two absolute values independently.

The historical journal reports a ten-day campaign, a clean chunk-boundary stop
and checkpoint resume after one disk-full incident caused by an unrelated job,
and an intact checkpoint followed by resume after one forced reboot.  The
available summaries, deterministic chunk checksums, periodic cross-checks, and
aggregate coverage comparison report no inconsistency.  They do not yet prove
the stronger phrase ``without data loss'': the release recovery must still
substantiate checkpoint continuity, the chunk~7 reconstruction, and the raw
artifact inventory.  These checks hash summaries, not every byte of output.
Candidate direct Rabung champions detected during the scan were frozen and passed to the
verification protocol of Section~\ref{s:bounds}; no bound was recorded from the
scanning pass alone.
