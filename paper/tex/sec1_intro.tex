\section{Introduction}\label{s:intro}

How far can a large search in the power-residue family push finite lower
bounds for van der Waerden numbers, and how can its output remain checkable
without trusting the search engine?  The two questions have different answers.
A GPU scan can discover useful primes quickly; the durable mathematical object
is instead the succinct triple $(p,r,k)$, whose finite Rabung hypotheses can be
re-established from scratch by a separate-source CPU verifier.  Only after
that pointwise certification do we use recurrence closure to decide whether a
direct-method improvement also wins a finite comparison in a dated,
primary-source-audited corpus.

For integers $r\ge2$ (colours) and $k\ge3$, the van der Waerden number
$W(r,k)$ is the least $N$ such that every $r$-colouring of $\{1,\dots,N\}$
contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $k$.  We put the
number of colours first.  Monroe~\cite{Monroe2026} uses the reverse convention
$W(k,r)$, while Fox and Hunter~\cite{FoxHunter2026} write $w(k;r)$.

The registered numerical claim contains nine direct three-colour Rabung
certificates, for $17\le k\le25$.  The headline certificate is
\[
  W(3,17)>31\,385\,622\,833,
\]
from $p=1\,961\,601\,427$, a factor $2.02$ over Monroe's published bound.
The fail-closed audit accepts all thirteen registered claim manifests, and
recurrence closure leaves the direct bounds as
the strongest values located in the registered corpus for $17\le k\le21$.
For $22\le k\le25$ they remain improvements within the direct Rabung family
but do not win the admitted comparison. Berlekamp's 1968 construction supplies
the winning value $W(3,24)>1\,082\,646\,556\,499$ and, by length monotonicity,
the same winning strict bound at $k=25$~\cite{Berlekamp1968}. This distinction
between a direct Rabung certificate and the strongest verified consequence in
the audited corpus is part of the claim, not a bibliographic
afterthought.

Three mechanisms make that distinction necessary.  The Folkman--Rabung
power-residue construction, in Rabung's published criterion~\cite{rabung1979},
turns a suitable prime $p$ into the direct bound
\[
  W(r,k)>(k-1)p+1.
\]
Blankenship, Cummings and Taranchuk~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bct2018}, by contrast,
prove the finite constructive recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bct-intro}
 W(r,k)>q\left(W\!\left(r-\left\lceil r/q\right\rceil,k\right)-1\right),
\end{equation}
for any prime $q\le k$. In the three-colour closure we take $q=q_k$, the
largest such prime; a documented strict bound $W(2,k)>B$ then implies
$W(3,k)>q_kB$. Xu's concatenation
recurrence~\cite[Theorem~4]{xu2013} supplies another closure operation, while
$W(r,k)>B$ implies $W(r,k+1)>B$ by elementary length monotonicity.  We therefore
compare bounds only after a machine-readable registry of published, archived,
and current inputs has been closed under all three operations.
Section~\ref{s:bounds}
states Xu's formula, the strict-integer convention and the finite corpus
policy explicitly.  The recent superexponential three-colour result of Fox and
Hunter~\cite{FoxHunter2026} and the asymptotic two-colour bound
$w(k)\ge(1-o(1))k2^{k-1}$ of Campos, Fox and
Schildkraut~\cite{CamposFoxSchildkraut2026} are separate asymptotic
developments. In the version-pinned sources audited here, neither supplies a
stronger explicit finite entry for the range compared below.
Gasarch and Haeupler's constructive review~\cite{GasarchHaeupler2011} provides
useful methodological context for separating existence, an efficient search,
and a checkable construction. Hunter's 2025 exponential improvement begins at
five colours~\cite{Hunter2025} and therefore does not alter the three-colour
finite table.

The historical campaign was configured to scan every prime in
$[9.7\times10^8,2\times10^9)$ and to compute the active profiles with $r\le9$.
Monroe's article describes a systematic scan limit of $9.5\times10^8$, while
its accompanying table lists the larger valid prime $969\,347\,371$; we use
the tabled value as the numerical hand-off.  Authenticated recovery now
accounts for all $515$ chunks: $514$ have matching journal and checkpoint
records, while chunk~7 is preserved by the checkpoint alone.  A separate
ordered-stream audit agrees byte for byte on every chunk.  These records
support coverage of the window and the empirical event list; they are
logically separate from pointwise verification of any particular triple.

Four additional registered triples are two-colour primes listed in an archived
phase of Monroe's Vdwnumbers.org BOINC project~\cite{MonroePhase2Archive}.
The earliest public evidence located in our audit attributes the values to that
project and its volunteer contributors. Their four final
manifests are accepted by the same local audit, which includes separate-source
CPU rechecks. We therefore claim re-verification, not their first discovery.
Applying
\eqref{eq:bct-intro} to these inputs gives the three-colour consequences for
$25\le k\le28$ displayed in Section~\ref{s:bounds}. A later author page
describes a scan to four billion~\cite{MonroePage}; our checker establishes the
validity of the listed triples, not gap-free coverage of that historical scan.

Computational power-residue lower bounds and cyclic-zipper extensions have
previously been pursued by Herwig, Heule, van Lambalgen and van
Maaren~\cite{herwig2007}, Rabung and Lotts~\cite{rabunglotts2012}, Liang, Xu,
Shao and Xiu~\cite{liang2012}, and
Monroe~\cite{Monroe2016v1,Monroe2017v4,Monroe2026}.
The priority review also traced Berlekamp's finite-field construction and a
stronger Landman--Robertson textbook statement whose proof status requires
separate treatment~\cite{Berlekamp1968,LandmanRobertson2004,LandmanRobertson2014,Johannson2007}.
Relative to that lineage, our contribution is the combination of direct
three-colour triples new to the audited corpus, a recurrence-closed and
provenance-bearing comparison, and a
stand-alone verification path whose arithmetic and source are separate from
the GPU engine.  We do not claim global optimality, exhaustive coverage beyond
the stated window, unqualified global priority for the direct triples, first
discovery of Monroe's archived primes, or a universal density law.

Section~\ref{s:bounds} states the registered numerical results, the recurrence
closure and their exact scope before the technical inventory.
Section~\ref{s:method} gives the Rabung criterion, proves the operational form
and explains the stand-alone certificate check.  Section~\ref{s:campaign}
describes discovery and the evidence needed for campaign coverage.  We conclude
with the scientific boundary and release contract.  The reconstructed holdout
record and its model-conditional analysis are separated into
Appendices~\ref{s:prereg}--\ref{s:densitydiagnostics}.
