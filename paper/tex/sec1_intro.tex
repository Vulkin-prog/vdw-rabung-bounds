\section{Introduction}\label{s:intro}

How far can a large search in the power-residue family push finite lower
bounds for van der Waerden numbers, and how can its output remain checkable
without trusting the search engine?  The two questions have different answers.
A GPU scan can discover useful primes quickly; the durable mathematical object
is instead the succinct triple $(p,r,k)$, whose finite Rabung hypotheses can be
re-established from scratch by a separate-source CPU verifier.  Only after
that pointwise certification do we use recurrence closure to decide whether a
direct-method improvement is also the strongest general bound in a dated
source corpus.

For integers $r\ge2$ (colours) and $k\ge3$, the van der Waerden number
$W(r,k)$ is the least $N$ such that every $r$-colouring of $\{1,\dots,N\}$
contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $k$.  We put the
number of colours first.  Monroe~\cite{Monroe2026} uses the reverse convention
$W(k,r)$, while Fox and Hunter~\cite{FoxHunter2026} write $w(k;r)$.

The registered release claim contains nine direct three-colour Rabung
certificates, for $17\le k\le25$.  The headline candidate is
\[
  W(3,17)>31\,385\,622\,833,
\]
from $p=1\,961\,601\,427$, a factor $2.02$ over Monroe's published bound.
Conditional on final acceptance of the thirteen claim manifests, recurrence
closure leaves the direct bounds as
the strongest values located in the registered corpus for $17\le k\le21$.
For $22\le k\le25$ they remain improvements within the direct Rabung family
but are dominated as general bounds. This distinction between the strongest
direct Rabung bound located in the dated corpus and its strongest general
consequence is part of the claim, not a bibliographic
afterthought.

Two mechanisms make that distinction necessary.  Rabung's power-residue
construction~\cite{rabung1979} turns a suitable prime $p$ into the direct bound
\[
  W(r,k)>(k-1)p+1.
\]
Blankenship, Cummings and Taranchuk~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bct2018}, by contrast,
prove the finite constructive recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bct-intro}
 W(r,k)>q\left(W\!\left(r-\left\lceil r/q\right\rceil,k\right)-1\right),
\end{equation}
where $q$ is the largest prime not exceeding $k$.  In particular, a documented
strict bound $W(2,k)>B$ implies $W(3,k)>qB$.  Xu's concatenation
recurrence~\cite{xu2013} supplies another closure operation.  We therefore
compare bounds only after a machine-readable registry of published, archived,
and current inputs has been closed under both recurrences. Section~\ref{s:bounds}
states Xu's formula, the strict-integer convention and the finite corpus
policy explicitly. The recent superexponential three-colour result of Fox and
Hunter~\cite{FoxHunter2026} and the asymptotic two-colour bound
$w(k)\ge(1-o(1))k2^{k-1}$ of Campos, Fox and
Schildkraut~\cite{CamposFoxSchildkraut2026} are separate asymptotic
developments; neither supplies a replacement numerical entry for the finite
range compared here.

The historical campaign was configured to scan every prime in
$[9.7\times10^8,2\times10^9)$ and to compute the active profiles with $r\le9$.
Monroe's article describes a systematic scan limit of $9.5\times10^8$, while
its accompanying table lists the larger valid prime $969\,347\,371$; we use
the tabled value as the numerical hand-off.  Recovery of the complete
checkpoint and ordered-prime evidence remains a release gate.  That recovery
supports claims about exhaustive coverage of the window and the empirical
event list; it is logically separate from pointwise verification of any
particular triple.

Four additional registered triples are two-colour primes listed in an archived
phase of Monroe's project~\cite{MonroePhase2Archive}.  Retained historical runs
report their acceptance by a separate-source checker, and the final raw
manifests remain open.  Priority for the primes is Monroe's.  Applying
\eqref{eq:bct-intro} to these inputs gives the three-colour consequences for
$25\le k\le28$ displayed in Section~\ref{s:bounds}.  The archive states that
the historical phase tested primes to four billion~\cite{MonroePage}; our
checker establishes the validity of the listed triples, not the exhaustiveness
of that historical scan.

Computational power-residue lower bounds and cyclic-zipper extensions have
previously been pursued by Herwig, Heule, van Lambalgen and van
Maaren~\cite{herwig2007}, Rabung and Lotts~\cite{rabunglotts2012}, Liang, Xu,
Shao and Xiu~\cite{liang2012}, and Monroe~\cite{Monroe2026}.  Relative to that
lineage, our contribution is the combination of new direct three-colour
triples, a recurrence-closed and provenance-bearing comparison, and a
stand-alone verification path whose arithmetic and source are separate from
the GPU engine.  We do not claim global optimality, exhaustive coverage beyond
the stated window, first discovery of Monroe's archived primes, or a universal
density law.

Section~\ref{s:bounds} states the registered numerical results, the recurrence
closure and their exact scope before the technical inventory.
Section~\ref{s:method} gives the Rabung criterion, proves the operational form
and explains the stand-alone certificate check. Section~\ref{s:campaign}
describes discovery and the evidence needed for campaign coverage. We conclude
with the scientific boundary and release contract. The reconstructed holdout
record and its model-conditional analysis are separated into
Appendices~\ref{s:prereg}--\ref{s:densitydiagnostics}.
