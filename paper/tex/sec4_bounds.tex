\subsection{Three colours: direct and recurrence-closed bounds}\label{ss:r3}

The historical scan produced nine candidate direct Rabung rows. They involve
only three distinct primes. By Lemma~\ref{lem:k-monotone}, the certificate
$(1\,999\,999\,927,3,19)$ implies all six rows with the same prime and
$20\le k\le25$. Thus only seven triples in the thirteen-row theorem are
mathematically primitive: three at three colours and the four two-colour
triples below. The release artifact nevertheless re-evaluates the complete
criterion at all thirteen registered triples as deliberate audit redundancy;
the six repeated checks are neither mathematically necessary nor counted as
independent implementation paths.

The headline value
\[
  W(3,17)>31\,385\,622\,833
\]
comes from $p=1\,961\,601\,427$ and improves Monroe's published
$15\,509\,557\,937$ by a factor $2.02$~\cite{Monroe2026}.  A direct
certificate is not automatically a general record: a published bound in
another colour column may imply a stronger result through a recurrence.

Let $q_k$ denote the largest prime not exceeding $k$.  From
Blankenship--Cummings--Taranchuk~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bct2018},
\[
  W(3,k)>q_k\bigl(W(2,k)-1\bigr).
\]
Their theorem permits every prime $q\le k$. For three colours, $q=2$ reduces
to the one-colour case, while every $q\ge3$ uses the same two-colour input;
choosing the largest such prime therefore loses no BCT consequence for the
three-colour outputs considered here.
If the registered input is the strict integer bound $W(2,k)>B$, then
$W(2,k)-1\ge B$, and the generated consequence is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bct-r3}
  W(3,k)>q_k B,
\end{equation}
with no off-by-one ambiguity.

For completeness, write $WR(s,k)$ for Xu's ring van der Waerden number: the
least $N$ such that, for every $n\ge N$, every $s$-colouring of
$\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ has a monochromatic $k$-term arithmetic progression.
Xu's concatenation theorem states that, for $k\ge3$, $s,t\ge2$, and
$5\le n<WR(s,k)$ whose least prime divisor exceeds $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xu}
  W(st,k)\ge n\bigl(W(t,k)-1\bigr)+1.
\end{equation}
Our registry stores strict integer bounds. Every ring witness $R$ used here
has least prime divisor greater than $k$, a precondition checked by the closure
tests. Hence inputs $WR(s,k)>R$ and $W(t,k)>B$ permit $n=R$ and give exactly
$W(st,k)>RB$, with no rounding or off-by-one convention left implicit.

The baseline program iterates the registered BCT specialization and
\eqref{eq:xu} to a fixed point. Its finite scope is $17\le k\le28$ and at most
four colours. At each step it retains exact integer bounds and a full
provenance path. Candidates are compared first by bound; numerical ties use
the deterministic order direct Rabung, published or archived seed, BCT, Xu,
one-colour exact, and finally the hash-derived node identifier. These
tie-breaks affect provenance only. Since both operators are monotone in their
input bounds, a dominated parent cannot improve a descendant. Iteration stops
when no winning node changes.

The comparison corpus was frozen on 28~August~2026 and consists exactly of the
seeds listed in \texttt{audit/baselines.json}: Monroe's published ordinary and
ring tables, the immutable direct tally distributed with his code, the four
archived phase-2 rows credited to Monroe, and the thirteen claims of this work.
Published entries require a stable primary locator. An unpublished or archived
entry is included only with an immutable source identity and explicit priority
attribution; a merely announced value is not silently promoted to a seed.
The generated output embeds this catalogue, SHA-256 hashes of both registries,
and the winning provenance paths. Accordingly, ``strongest'' below always
means strongest consequence of these explicitly listed inputs, not global
optimality over an unspecified literature corpus.

\subsection{Archived two-colour inputs and recurrence consequences}\label{ss:r2}

After the scan, we located a table from a second, unpublished phase of Monroe's
BOINC project (2021, two colours only)~\cite{MonroePhase2Archive}.  The project
page states that this phase pushed the checked range from one billion to four
billion~\cite{MonroePage}; the largest prime actually listed in the recovered
table is $3\,478\,685\,143$.  We credit the listed primes to Monroe and use
our checker only to test whether the four displayed triples satisfy Rabung's
criterion.

These four inequalities are theorem consequences of the corresponding
two-colour inputs, not additional GPU discoveries.  Priority for the primes
remains Monroe's.  Xu's recurrence is included in the automatic closure but
does not improve any of these three-colour winners.  Final release-level
reproducibility remains conditional on the manifest protocol of
Section~\ref{ss:protocol}.

For $k=25$ the campaign summaries identify the same last candidate in
$[9.7\times10^8,2\times10^9)$ as the archived table; complete campaign
recovery and ordered-prime identity remain release gates.  For $k=26,27,28$
our campaign-range candidates are weaker, and the displayed archive primes
were checked directly in retained historical runs.  None of this establishes
that Monroe's phase exhaustively tested every prime up to four billion: that is
an attributed archival statement, not a consequence of the recovered table.

The source is the Internet Archive capture of 28~April~2022 of
\url{http://vdwnumbers.org/bounds.txt}~\cite{MonroePhase2Archive}; its header

URL, both dates, source-repository blob identity, and SHA-256 in
\path{publication/baseline-provenance.json}.  It does not redistribute the
third-party table absent permission.  Once the final manifests and external
execution records are complete, the defensible novelty claim is a
separately auditable re-verification with a public stand-alone checker and
complete logs---not first discovery of the primes and not a newly audited claim
of historical scan coverage.
