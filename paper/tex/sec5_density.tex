\section{An empirical density comparison}\label{s:density}

The campaign classified each tested prime by the run-valid predicate and
preserved the survivor event list and aggregate counts, but not the complete
per-prime max-run profile.  This section uses those retained data for a deliberately narrow purpose: a prospective
forecast comparison for the run-valid $(3,17)$ primes beyond a fixed training
cut.  It does not promote the comparison to a theorem, a universal density
law, or a proof that any Rabung search has been exhausted.  All probabilistic
statements below are conditional on the explicitly stated count model.

\subsection{Population and mirror reference}\label{ss:runlaw}

Call a prime \emph{run-valid} for $(r,k)$ when condition~(a) holds, that is,
when $\operatorname{maxrun}(p,r)<k$, and \emph{valid} when
$(a)\wedge(b)$ holds.  The statistical estimand in this section is the
run-valid population.  The bounds of Section~\ref{s:bounds}, by contrast, use
the full criterion.  Condition~(b) depends on $k$ and is not represented in the
versioned run-valid event list used below; it must therefore not be silently
absorbed into a common density correction.

The coloring satisfies
\[
  c(p-x)=c(x)+c(-1) \pmod r.
\]
Consequently only the first $(p-1)/2$ positions are free.  The natural
i.i.d.\ reference for the maximal run therefore has length
$n=(p-1)/2$, not $p-1$.  Descriptively, this accounts for most of the shift in
the natural reference length.  We make no numerical mean-run claim here,
because the versioned event list records survivors rather than the full
per-prime max-run profile.

For $r=3$ the case $c(-1)=r/2$ cannot occur, since $r$ is odd; more generally
that case exists only for even $r$.

\subsection{Bare reference and domain}\label{ss:barelaw}

With $(p-1)/2$ free positions, the elementary Poisson-clumping reference is
\[
  \Lambda_{r,k}(p)=\frac{(r-1)(p-1)}{2r^k},
  \qquad
  g_{0}(p)=e^{-\Lambda_{r,k}(p)}.
\]
It motivates the qualitative scale $p\asymp k r^k$ at which direct Rabung
survivors become rare.  We do not use the local fit below to quote precise
numbers of future primes, to declare a well exhausted, or to assign narrow
uncertainty intervals far outside the fitted window.

The program frozen during the campaign used the operational convention
\[
  \widetilde\Lambda_{r,k}(p)=\frac{(r-1)p}{2r^k},
  \qquad
  \widetilde g_0(p)=e^{-\widetilde\Lambda_{r,k}(p)}.
\]
Thus $\widetilde\Lambda-\Lambda=(r-1)/(2r^k)$.  We retain the tilded
convention below so that the holdout calculation is exactly the one that was
frozen, while keeping $\Lambda$ as the natural half-length reference.

\begin{remark}[Domain of the empirical comparison]\label{r:domain}
The two corrected candidates considered below are
\[
  \widetilde g_{1}(p)=e^{-\widetilde\Lambda_{3,17}(p)}
            +m e^{-\beta\widetilde\Lambda_{3,17}(p)},
  \qquad
  \widetilde g_{2}(p)=e^{-\widetilde\Lambda_{3,17}(p)}+\eta,
\]
with $m=8\cdot10^{-4}$, $\beta=1/2$, and
$\eta=1.62\cdot10^{-5}$.  These are local intensity models for condition~(a)
in the observed deep-tail window.  In particular $\widetilde g_1(p)\to1+m>1$ as
$\widetilde\Lambda\to0$, so it is not a global probability law.  The numerical
parameters were selected on the training window; freezing them prevents
holdout tuning but does not remove their estimation uncertainty.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Prospective forecast comparison}\label{ss:eta}

After completion of chunk $180$, when $1727$ run-valid $(3,17)$ primes had
been observed, the three numerical candidates
$\widetilde g_0,\widetilde g_1,\widetilde g_2$ were fixed.  The
four scheduled checkpoint values $250,300,400,515$ were recorded before the
first read.  The versioned $1859$-entry list, labelled complete by the historical
analysis, reconstructs the count in every campaign chunk conditional on that
list.

Let
\[
 I_j=[970\,000\,000+(j-1)\,2\,000\,000,
      970\,000\,000+j\,2\,000\,000)
\]
and, for a set $A$ of chunks, define the corrected mean derived from a frozen
intensity,
\[
  \mu_\ell(A)=\sum_{j\in A}\int_{I_j}
       \frac{\widetilde g_\ell(p)}{2\log p}\,dp,
  \qquad \ell\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]
The factor $1/(2\log p)$ is the analytic density used for primes
$p\equiv1\pmod3$.  Table~\ref{tab:milestones} uses the four \emph{disjoint}
increments as its primary view.

The corresponding cumulative observations are $90,110,126,132$.  They are
four views of the same trajectory, not four replications.  The fixed decaying
forecast is close to the observed total, the frozen constant-floor candidate
substantially overpredicts it, and the bare reference underpredicts it.

\begin{remark}[Frozen readout and post hoc diagnostics]\label{r:sigma}
Three layers must be kept distinct.

First, the frozen inputs were the three intensity functions, their numerical
parameters, the baseline count, and---before the first scheduled read---the
reading times.  Second, the live tool selected the closest displayed mean and
printed Wald-type discrepancies $(N-\mu)/\sqrt{N}$ for the bare and
constant-floor candidates.  That was the original prospective readout.  It
did not compute deviance residuals or exact Poisson tails; two manually noted
standardizations for the decaying candidate were later found to use the wrong
denominator.

Third, all deviance, dispersion, autocorrelation, negative-binomial,
and block calculations are retrospective diagnostics.  The archival script
also used chunk midpoints as the two endpoints of a cumulative integral.
Table~\ref{tab:milestones} corrects that endpoint convention by integrating the
full observed chunk intervals.  This changes no observation and no frozen
operational intensity function; it changes only the integration limits.  The
corrected numerical means are therefore post-campaign recalculations of the
frozen functions.  Replacing the operational $\widetilde\Lambda=p/3^{17}$ by
the natural $\Lambda=(p-1)/3^{17}$ changes every holdout total by less than
$9\cdot10^{-7}$ and changes no cell of Table~\ref{tab:milestones} at two
decimal places.

The four increments are non-overlapping.  They are independent only under the
specified inhomogeneous-Poisson model; disjointness alone is not evidence of
independence.  Accordingly, we do not promote a conditional Poisson tail
probability as a model-robust significance level.
\end{remark}

The versioned event list permits limited retrospective checks.  On the $335$
post-freeze chunks, the frozen decaying model gives Pearson statistic
$X^2=324.2$ (quasi-Poisson scale $X^2/335=0.968$); an NB2 sensitivity fit puts
the additional-dispersion parameter at the Poisson boundary, and the Poisson
deviance is $210.46$.  The Pearson
residuals have Ljung--Box $Q_{12}=7.17$ (asymptotic conditional reference
$p=0.85$), and aggregation into blocks of $5,10,20$ chunks gives Pearson
scales $0.81,0.96,1.05$.  These checks reveal no gross overdispersion or serial
correlation for that fixed model, but they remain post hoc and have limited
power in a sparse, non-stationary tail.  A moving-block resampling of the
per-chunk log-score difference (circular blocks of length $10$, $20\,000$
seeded replicates) gives a mean decaying-minus-bare advantage of $0.01185$ per
chunk, with percentile interval $[-0.00509,0.02957]$ and bootstrap fraction
$0.092$ at or below zero.  For decaying minus the frozen constant-floor model,
the corresponding interval is $[0.31298,0.49363]$ and no replicate is
non-positive.  Thus the former comparison is less decisive than the latter.
We therefore report the
forecast ordering, not a universal fitted family or a discovery-level
$p$-value.

The scanner changed earlier in the campaign, but the final source fingerprint
begins at chunk $79$.  Thus all chunks after the forecast cut use the same
scanner fingerprint.  This removes scanner-source changes as a confounder for the
prospective comparison, while whole-campaign descriptive aggregates still
require the revision table discussed in Section~\ref{ss:execution}.

\subsection{Summary}\label{ss:densitysummary}

The mirror symmetry supplies a useful first-order reference, and one fixed
decaying correction tracked the held-out $(3,17)$ trajectory more closely than
the bare and constant-floor candidates.  The result is an empirically useful
forecast check for one target and one finite window.  It is not four
independent confirmations, not a proof of a universal two-exponential law,
and not a quantitative certificate that any future Rabung search is empty.
